Finding one insect in a moving bush — and hearing its wings

An event camera reports nothing but change: no frames, no exposure, just a stream of per-pixel events at microsecond resolution. Track individual insects against wind-blown vegetation, then read each animal's wingbeat frequency from the same stream.

Section pattern

The problem, watched directly

Vegetation moves constantly, and to a change-detecting sensor it is loud. What separates an insect is not brightness but kinematics: vegetation oscillates in place while an insect goes somewhere.

Design rule

The combined score is the weakest link — the minimum normalised margin across criteria — so a track cannot buy its way past a failed test with a large margin elsewhere.

Prose pattern

Longer academic prose is set in the serif: event sensors report per-pixel brightness changes at microsecond resolution with over 120 dB of dynamic range, so they do not blur on a fast wingbeat and do not saturate across a sun-shade boundary.
