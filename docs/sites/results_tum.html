A second environment is only useful if it can change conclusions; this one did, in one place.

2 · The ladder, third scene

Whitening's retrieval cost is scene-dependent. The dissociation is not.

gap 30 (1 s) gap 90 (3 s ≈ classroom's gap-300 as a fraction) gap 300 (10 s ≈ half the sequence)

TUM ladder (yolov8n crops, chance 0.168). The classroom's monotone decline does not appear: whiten-32 is the best rung here (+0.024 over raw at the fraction-matched gap), k=128 is a wash, and only full ZCA clearly costs. Note the fraction-matching: this sequence is 20.4 s, so gap 300 excludes half of it — the honest cross-scene column is gap 90.

What survives, said precisely. Across three scenes the k=128 whitening penalty is −0.097 (classroom), −0.004 (school_run1), −0.008 (TUM): the dramatic classroom penalty is the outlier, and the general claim must be "the retrieval cost of whitening is scene-dependent; centring and z-scoring are retrieval-safe everywhere; full ZCA always costs."

The dissociation is intact and sharper: the four IsoScore-1.000 rungs span 0.440 to 0.493 while raw (IsoScore 0.031) sits at 0.476, inside that span. Perfect isotropy is compatible with the best and the worst retrieval on the chart — isotropy predicts nothing about key quality, which is the paper's actual claim.

4 · The critical test

Through the memory, held out — the sharpest claim faces its second scene

The paper's most quotable finding — whitening scores below chance on held-out data — was measured through the memory on the classroom only. TUM's raw-cosine result above (no whitening penalty) makes this the test that matters: if the through-memory harm reproduces here despite the benign cosine picture, the harm comes from the memory pipeline itself, which is a finding; if it does not, the claim is classroom-only and gets hedged.

Verdict: the test collapsed here, and the diagnostics say why — the answer is "wrong trajectory type", not "centring is bad". The mocap space is a 2.42 × 1.34 m desk orbit (oracle 0.08 m, constant predictor 0.50 m). Every real treatment scores below the constant; "raw" leads only by being a constant predictor itself (1–3 distinct cells — the same degeneracy flag that caught it on the classroom).

The mechanism: on a handheld orbit, what the camera sees is governed by viewing direction, not position — and this memory binds appearance to position only. Nearly identical positions produce wildly different views, so appearance→position recall is ill-posed on this trajectory regardless of treatment. Two statements do survive: whitening is still the worst treatment on both scenes (−172% to −196% here), and the classroom's "whitening below chance" claim stays classroom-only until a fair second scene exists.

What a fair second scene needs: either a locomotion-style trajectory where position drives appearance (e.g. a larger-translation sequence), or the memory must bind orientation as well — the circular heading encoder already exists (ctx_head), so appearance ⊗ S(x,y) ⊗ H(ψ) is a small extension, and it is the named next test.
