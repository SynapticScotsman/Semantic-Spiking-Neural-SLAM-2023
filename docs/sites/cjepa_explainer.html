C-JEPA: hide one object, force the model to ask the others

“Causal-JEPA: Learning World Models through Object-Level Latent Masking” — Nam, Le Lidec, Maes, LeCun & Balestriero. One idea carries the paper: a world model only learns interactions if you make interactions the only way to lower its loss. They do that by deleting whole objects from the model's view of the past and asking it to reconstruct them anyway.

+20 ptsabsolute, counterfactual video QA (CLEVRER) vs the same model without object masking
1.02%of a patch-based world model's input features, at comparable Push-T control
8×faster model-predictive control than patch tokens
0 pixelspredicted — everything happens between latent vectors

1 · The pipeline

Frames become objects; objects become the only tokens

A frozen encoder (Slot Attention over frozen DINOv2 features) turns each video frame into a handful of object slots — one small vector per thing in the scene. The world model never sees pixels or patches again: its entire input is a few object vectors per timestep, plus optional action/proprioception tokens. That is where the 1% token count and the 8× planning speedup come from — attention is quadratic, and five objects is a much smaller set than a few hundred patches.

The encoder is frozen, and the loss is a plain L2 between predicted and target latents. No pixel reconstruction, and no collapse-prevention machinery — collapse is impossible because the targets never move. (This is the DINO-WM / V-JEPA pattern, not the LeJEPA/SIGReg pattern, despite the shared authorship.)

2 · The idea

Masking one object's whole history is an intervention, not augmentation

Ordinary masked prediction hides random patches, and a model can fill those in from local texture. C-JEPA instead hides one object's entire slot trajectory across the history window — every timestep except a single identity anchor at the start, kept so the model knows which object it must reconstruct. Now nothing about that object's past is available. The only route to its state is through the other objects: if the red ball is hidden but the blue one suddenly changes direction, something collided with it.

Masking off — the predictor can see everything.
object A object B object C auxiliaries (actions)

Toggle the mask. With it on, C's cells become unknowns (the ringed cell is the identity anchor z̃ = φ(zt0) + eτ), and the model's only evidence is the arrows — other objects and the actions. The paper's phrase for this is a counterfactual-like query: “given everything else, what must C have been doing?” Future tokens (t+1) are always masked; that part is ordinary forward prediction.

3 · Why it works

The theorem, in one sentence

Define an object's influence neighborhood: the smallest set of other entities whose history fully determines what you can know about the hidden object (a Markov-blanket flavoured notion). Theorem 1: the predictor that minimises the masked loss must use that neighborhood — any model that ignores it pays strictly higher loss. Interaction use stops being a nice-to-have and becomes arithmetic: it is the only way down the loss surface.

What “causal” does and does not mean here — their own words. Remark 1 of the paper is explicitly modest: “causal” means temporally directed predictive dependencies that stay stable under masking, “rather than claims of causal identifiability.” The model learns who predicts whom under intervention on observability, not a verified causal graph. Quote them at that strength and no stronger.

4 · Why this matters to the VSA work

Three places their story and ours are the same story

1 · Entity granularity — their premise, our measurement

C-JEPA's founding claim is that patch/frame tokens are the wrong unit and object latents are the right one. We measured the memory-side version of exactly that: a frame-level embedding makes per-object cueing impossible in principle (88.6% of our detections share their vector with another class), and per-object crops retrieve at 0.720 where frames manage 0.435. Their training argument and our storage measurement are two views of one claim.

2 · Masked completion ≡ partial-cue retrieval

“Infer the hidden object from the visible context objects” is precisely the query type of the episodic-memory pivot (“the thing that was next to the kettle”). C-JEPA trains a predictor to answer it; a VSA memory computes it by unbinding the context from a superposition — no training. Same question, opposite mechanisms: learned dynamics vs algebraic recall. That contrast is a paragraph, not a rivalry.

3 · Their identity anchor is binding, done additively

Slots are permutation-equivariant, so a masked cell must be told which object it stands for: z̃ = φ(identity) + etime — identity added to a time code. A VSA gets the same guarantee by construction: identity ⊗ state, with an exact inverse. Where they engineer identity preservation into the architecture, binding supplies it as algebra.

4 · A contrast our WP4 result makes sharp

Our SIGReg head failed off-distribution because its BatchNorm statistics were distribution-bound (the latent's shared mean reappeared, χ 94 vs 80 raw). C-JEPA never risks that failure: it predicts in the frozen encoder's own space and applies no trained normalisation to anything downstream. A useful sentence for the mechanism section — and a reason their slot latents are a clean candidate for our geometry measurements (χ, Timkey, held-out) once the draft is frozen.
