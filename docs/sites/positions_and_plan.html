Four points of view, and the plan

what each collaborator is actually proposing · where they agree · the one real disagreement · what happens next · 2026-08-10

the short version

Everyone agrees on the substrate: phasor vectors, bind and bundle and unbind, fractional power encoding for space and time. What differs is what the vector is for. One view says it replaces the scene graph. Two say it sits alongside one. One says the algebra already expresses what the scene graphs compute. They are not in conflict so much as at different distances from the robot.

the four views

view 1 · the ICRA draft (ours)

The vector replaces the structure

"Throw the structure away. Keep one 32 KB vector. Measure what still works."

the claimThe scene graph and the trace are opposite design points. We characterise the trace standing on its own: how it relocalises, how it grounds objects, how it merges, how its interference scales.

strongest cardIt is finished and measured. Benchmarks exist, the merge result is exact and checkable, the capacity law makes predictions that came true.

weakest cardIt concedes what graphs are good at (exact instance identity, arbitrary relational queries), so a reviewer hunting for ambition can call the scope narrow.

Answers: how much can a fixed-size vector still do?

view 2 · Naitri

The vector is an overlay on the structure

"The graph knows where things are now. The vector remembers where they have been."

the claimKeep SuperMap or FARM authoritative for current state, identity and geometry. Add a fixed-budget VSA holding the approximate history, which returns candidates the graph then verifies exactly.

strongest cardIt does not fight the graph where the graph wins. The claim that at fixed memory it retains more useful history than a truncated or sampled log is testable, and nobody owns it yet.

weakest cardIt depends on somebody else's system running. SuperMap's code is unreleased; FARM's is released.

Answers: at a fixed memory budget, who remembers more of what happened?

view 3 · Lorin

Isolate the backend and race them

"Same eyes, different memory, same test."

the claimOne perception front end, two map backends (4D scene graph versus SSP/VSA), the same navigation task suite. Plus hybrid variants: the SSP as a fast index into the graph, as a replacement for edge and existence tracking, or as the per-node confidence.

strongest cardThis is exactly the fair comparison already demanded here (same front end, different backend), and its metrics — footprint, query big-O, wall-clock, update cost — are the axes where the trace actually wins.

weakest cardIt needs a working task suite and substantial integration engineering before it produces a single number.

Answers: holding perception fixed, does the representation change the outcome?

view 4 · Shay

The algebra already expresses what they compute

"Their mechanisms are our identities."

the claimAn equation-by-equation mapping from SuperMap and FARM onto FHRR: 6 exact correspondences, 4 bounded approximations, 10 reformulations, and no weak analogies.

strongest cardIt is the intellectual case for the whole programme, and it hands over four concrete upgrades we do not currently have (listed below).

weakest cardIt is theory until an experiment runs, and one of its recommendations contradicts one of our measurements.

Answers: is the correspondence real, and what does it buy over a hand-written predicate library?

agreement and divergence

✓ all four agree

  • The state should be fixed-size, not growing.
  • Relations should be computed at query time, not stored. FARM independently made the same choice, so this is agreement, not advantage.
  • Comparisons must hold the perception front end fixed.
  • The case is strongest on systems axes (bytes, update cost, query cost), not on raw accuracy.

✕ where they split

Replace (view 1) versus overlay (views 2 and 3b).

Our ICRA draft argues the trace as an alternative to the graph. The collaborators are converging on the trace as a component inside or beside one.

These are not contradictory: you cannot argue the overlay credibly without first knowing what the bounded map does alone, which is what the draft measures. But they are different papers with different claims, and the framing needs deciding rather than drifting.

the one real technical disagreement

Shay's pipeline whitens semantic embeddings before fractional power encoding. Our measured rule is centre or z-score, never whiten (−47.6% signal on the classroom over 5 seeds, though harmless on chess). They may not actually conflict, and the distinction is precise:

what gets whitenedhow it is then used
our measurementthe content vector as a binding key — bound directly into the trace
Shay's pipelinethe semantic embedding as an argument to the encoder — fractional-power-encoded first, then bound
Why this matters and why it is cheap. Shay's own argument is that FPE outputs are quasi-orthogonal by construction, so the pathology whitening fixes is a different one in his case. That is a one-day experiment, and it should run before either claim is written down as general. It is the only item on this page that could change a sentence already in the paper.

what Shay's note gives us that we do not have

ideawhat it fixescost
Submap-local frames Our standing shared-frame merge caveat. A loop closure updates one submap-to-world transform instead of invalidating every stored position. cheap — turns an open problem into a known fix
Fusion update + the κ statistic Re-observation currently grows N. Remove-and-replace is exact, so N counts objects. κ (the bundle norm) is a threshold-free data-association and change score. 1–2 days, strong journal material
Residue (CRT) codes Separates the range budget from the crosstalk budget, which our capacity law currently conflates. Also the best grid-cell anchor in the programme. medium — journal scope
Structured orthogonal random features Kills the phase-matrix storage (~12 MB at d=4096), the same embarrassment as our "decoder grids dominate the honest map state" note. small, strictly better on both axes

Two convergences worth telling him about, because they are independent derivations of the same thing: his participation-ratio effective load is the PR term in our measured capacity law, and his sequential extraction under decreasing noise is our matching-pursuit result.

C · The T-RO journal version

T-RO explicitly accepts "evolutionary" papers: extended versions of your own conference paper with substantial new results. The material deliberately cut from ICRA is that delta, now with the collaborator additions folded in.

  • Frame recovery inside the algebra, plus submap-local frames, measured
  • K-scaling from 2 to 16 robots, and the bandwidth protocol
  • The formal capacity derivation, including the range-versus-crosstalk split that residue codes make explicit
  • The full ConceptGraphs head-to-head across all eight scenes
  • The fusion update and κ
  • Structured orthogonal random features, which fixes the honest map-state number
  • The relational layer, if the stopping rule is legalised
parallel · not ours to lead

D · The collaborators' paper

Naitri's overlay plus Lorin's head-to-head is a second paper. Our contribution to it is the trace, the capacity law, and the index-not-store architecture; Shay's validation ladder (rungs 1 to 3) is its spine.

Practical note. FARM's code and FARM-Scenes are released; SuperMap's are not. If a hybrid target has to be picked to make progress this year, FARM is the one that can actually be run.
