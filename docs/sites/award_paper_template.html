The award-paper template

What ICRA, RSS, CoRL and T-RO award winners from 2022–2025 actually do — section budgets, narrative spine, title and abstract construction — distilled into a revision guide for the 32 KB map paper. Eighteen agents read the papers themselves rather than their abstracts; every pattern below is tagged by how well the evidence supports it.

First: three ICRA 2027 rules, verified against the CFP — one corrects earlier advice

The page limit is 8 pages including references, and extra pages cannot be bought. Our icra6.pdf compiles to exactly 8 pages with the bibliography in. It is already compliant. I previously said it needed roughly two pages of trims — that was wrong, and it was my error rather than the draft's. The work below is therefore reallocation inside 8 pages, not amputation.

Review is double-anonymous. The draft currently carries a full five-author block with affiliations, a Telluride acknowledgement and named grant numbers — all of which must be stripped for submission. This also kills one piece of award-paper advice outright: winners put project URLs and code links in the Fig. 1 caption, and you cannot.

AI-tool use must be disclosed in the acknowledgements, and no supplementary PDF is permitted — a video (≤20 MB, ≤180 s) is the only attachment. Nothing load-bearing may live outside the 8 pages, so the “exile the derivation to an appendix” move that MAC-VO used at ICRA and CoRL papers use freely is not available to us. The skeleton

Eight IEEEtran pages is sixteen columns. References take ~1.25, floats (hero, law figure, head-to-head figure, four tables) take ~4.5, leaving ~10 columns of body text. Budgets below are scaled to that; the “what winners do” column is where the value is.

SectionBudgetWhat award papers do here
Title + abstract0.5 5–8 sentences (F3RM won CoRL best paper with five). The contribution sentence lands no later than sentence 4. One headline number early — Diffusion Policy puts 46.9% in sentence 2 — and every other number is relocated to the Fig. 1 caption.
Hero figure~1 A teaser result drawn from the system's own state, not an architecture block. MAC-VO shows the map built by its own optimiser; Kimera-Multi puts “2188 m” in the caption. Captions run 40–85 words and pre-empt the top objection inline (RACER: “no external computer is used”).
I. Introduction1.25 Three to six paragraphs closing on ≤4 refutable contribution bullets with forward section refs (MAC-VO uses three). NoMaD proves two paragraphs suffice when the teaser is strong. Reach the specific gap within three or four sentences.
II. Related Work0.75 Deliberately thin. MAC-VO spends ~19% of its body text here and won both the overall and perception awards; the ICRA 2023 overall winner has no Related Work section at all. One contrast per paragraph, F3RM-style: “Unlike the multi-scale features used in LERF, we…”
III. Method2.5 The largest block, but every subsection must be a stage of one mechanism. MAC-VO's four subsections all serve its uncertainty model; TinyMPC's background teaches exactly the three tools its solver combines and nothing else.
IV. Capacity law1.75 One formal statement in a display box, placed where the objection lives. Every theory-carrying winner does this — ergodic search Theorem 1, KnowNo Propositions 1–2. Assert measurements flatly; hedge mechanism and universality explicitly.
V. Experiments3 A claims ledger: AESOP declares H1–H5, tags section titles, and closes each with “this validates Hn”; ergodic search pre-registers Q1–Q6. Setup folds into bold run-in heads (Metrics. Baselines.) rather than its own section.
VI. Conclusion & Discussion0.5 A standalone Limitations heading is atypical: seven of eight ICRA winners read have none, and the exception merges it (Code as Policies: “Discussion and Limitations”). Limitations carry numbers and are phrased as scope-with-a-path.

The one structural change worth making

Our draft has a standalone Limitations section and a separate Experimental Setup. Folding Limitations into “Conclusion & Discussion” and dissolving Setup into run-in heads matches the winners and frees roughly a column — which, now that we are not fighting a page limit, buys space for the predicted-vs-measured figure rather than being cut.

The narrative spine, written for this paper

The arc award papers follow, instantiated with our own measured results. These are candidate sentences to adapt, not filler — and once a number appears here it should appear verbatim everywhere else in the paper.

  1. Name the enemy — never open on a statistic A robot's semantic map is usually a structure that grows: a node per object, a row per landmark, a descriptor per keyframe. Growth obligates machinery — association to update it, correspondence to merge it, traversal code to query it.
  2. The turn — why the obvious fix fails Capping the structure is not by itself an answer: the field's own survey indicts bounded maps because quality “degrades silently when scene complexity approaches table capacity.”
  3. “We present” — mechanism inside the sentence, as contrast Instead of a growing structure, the map is one fixed 32 KB vector: insertion is a multiply-add, a query is one unbind, and merging two robots' maps is elementwise addition — exact, with zero association decisions.
  4. The quotable law — the paper's ping Superposition interference, the price of writing everything into one vector, follows an empirical capacity law that predicted a held-out condition to 1.4% and diagnosed a deployed failure before we found its fix.
  5. Witnesses, with the steelman visible We test the law on three public witnesses, each after strengthening the opposition: relocalisation on 7-Scenes against exact nearest neighbour over the same descriptors at ~1000× the bytes; grounding on all eight Replica scenes against an explicit-instance ceiling; and a head-to-head under ConceptGraphs' own scorer, its published result reproduced to 99% first.
  6. The payoff — including the honest loss The fixed budget loses on clean input (0.80×) and overtakes the unbounded map in all nine degraded-input conditions tested: it degrades on the schedule the law sets rather than silently.
  7. Coin the thesis, then close Predicted degradation is what a fixed budget buys: bytes fixed before deployment, update cost independent of map age, merge cost independent of map content, and failure modes given by an equation rather than discovered in the field.

Coin one phrase and repeat it verbatim

Winners give the community a citable handle and never paraphrase it: uncertainty alignment (KnowNo), metrics-aware vs scale-agnostic (MAC-VO), the translation problem (Translating Images). Our draft currently uses three different phrasings of its own thesis. Pick one — “predicted degradation is what a fixed budget buys” is the strongest — and use exactly those words at all four stations: abstract, close of the law section, head-to-head, conclusion.

The opening kit

Title

Median award title: 8 words. Zero winners sampled use “Towards”, “A Novel” or “A Framework for”; none carries a numeral. Two winning constructions: a pronounceable name with a colon (Diffusion Policy, MAC-VO, Kimera-Multi, NoMaD), or a territory-grab claim with no colon at all (Robot Parkour Learning, Time Optimal Ergodic Search). Our current title is 16 words and enumerates three claims where winners encode one.

Abstract, seven sentences with fixed roles

S1 enemy (growth) · S2 gap as the survey's own quote · S3 “we present” + mechanism + 32 KB · S4 the law + 1.4% · S5 witnesses with the steelman clause · S6 the crossover, 0.80× clean and all nine degraded conditions · S7 the coined thesis.

The single highest-value edit in this document

Our abstract carries roughly twelve numerals (D=8192, 64 KB, 0.36, 0.29, 32.8 MB, 10−15, 622, 84/89%, 1.4%, 44→62). The openings sweep found award abstracts in this genre carry one headline number early, with identity numbers pushed to the hero caption. Keep 32 KB (identity), 1.4% (result) and “all nine” (a count — the counts-as-authority move); move the rest out. And add the crossover sentence: the thesis's own proof is currently missing from the abstract while merge micro-numbers are present.

Hero-figure contract

Our hero is already the right species — a teaser result drawn from the map's own state, with an objection pre-empted in the caption (“no data association whatsoever”). Three fixes: compress the caption from ~200 words toward the 40–85 winners use; open it with the falsifiable claim rather than a description; and normalise the numbers the way Parkour does (“0.40 m = 1.53× robot height”). judgment Consider making one panel the predicted-vs-measured interference curve with the held-out point marked 1.4% — the law is the contribution but no panel currently shows it, and KnowNo's headline figure is its deviation plot.

Style rules that separate winners from the median

  • One ping, three witnesses. attested Winners show one derivation instantiated three ways (F3RM's grasp/place/language). Write relocalisation, grounding and the head-to-head as three tests of the law's prediction, not three independent results.
  • Claim = number + named baseline + named condition + section ref. attested MAC-VO: “61.9% over DROID-SLAM in trel”. Never a bare percentage.
  • Freeze numbers verbatim across abstract, intro and results. attested PoliFormer's +28.5 is identical in all three. Audit ours: 1.4%, 0.36/0.29 m, ~1000×, 32 KB, 0.80×, 9/9, 99%.
  • Denominate claims in the constrained resource. attested TinyMPC prices everything in kB and ms against hard limits and states its own capacity ceiling as a first-class number. Bytes belong in every results table, including the ConceptGraphs one.
  • Report errors against floors and exchange rates. attested IRP puts 2.6 cm real error next to 2.3 cm pixel size. “0.36 vs 0.29 m” should never appear without “at ~1000× fewer bytes” in the same sentence.
  • Steelman the baseline, then say you did. attested KnowNo re-tunes the baseline's threshold before comparing; IRP hands its rival ground-truth parameters. Our 99% reproduction is this culture — state it as a methodological contribution in its own sentence, not as setup.
  • Attribute failures with counts and mechanisms. attested F3RM prints 13/19 language failures and names CLIP's bag-of-words behaviour as the cause. Our miss taxonomy and the 62/38 forensics already speak this language.
  • Hedge mechanism and universality, never measurements. attested The empirical-law papers assert what they measured flatly, then mark speculation explicitly and show distributions rather than means.
  • Anticipate the reviewer in-text. attested PoliFormer uses a bold run-in question (“Has PoliFormer performance saturated?”). Budget three or four sentences for “is 32 KB special or arbitrary?” and “does the law survive distribution shift?”
  • Voice: measured, zero superlatives. attested Award citations reward “rigor… convincing results on challenging benchmarks” — never novelty or SOTA margins. MAC-VO's citation is one sentence about an uncertainty model and convincing results.

What not to do

  • Title padding (“Towards”, “A Novel”, “A Framework for”), or enumerating three claims in a title.
  • Scattering five or more metrics through the abstract — the sweep calls this median-submission behaviour explicitly, and our draft does it.
  • Closing an abstract with “experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method”. No winner sampled does.
  • Terse figure captions. Every winner's caption is a 40–85-word mini-abstract.
  • A bare architecture block diagram as Fig. 1.
  • Multiple vague “first” claims — winners make at most one, tightly scoped.
  • Mechanism-free verbs in claims: provides, enables, allows.
  • Hiding losses. F3RM prints 31/50 and “far from practical for industrial use” in its camera-ready headline table — and won. A loss with a one-sentence mechanistic diagnosis becomes a demonstration of the thesis.

Caveats — read before trusting any of this

  • Survivorship bias is unaddressed. Every pattern here is conditioned on winning. Without a matched sample of rejected papers we cannot separate what causes awards from what every competent paper does (teaser figures, three contribution bullets) or from lab-brand confounds.
  • Awards and acceptance are different games. Awards come from a small committee after acceptance; our binding gate is three anonymous reviewers. Award-optimal boldness and acceptance-optimal defensiveness are correlated, not identical — and only the former is in this corpus.
  • The sample skews to learning and perception systems papers from US labs. Our true genre peers — compact map representations, VSA/HDC, memory-bounded SLAM — are absent, and the SLAM/mapping reviewers we will actually draw expect ATE/RPE tables and ORB-SLAM3-family baselines that award-committee rhetoric says nothing about.
  • Roughly half the corpus is RSS/CoRL/T-RO. Several of their signature moves (hypothesis ledgers, related-work-after-results, long appendices) have zero verified ICRA-winner instances. Treat the ledger idea as imported, and remember appendices are unavailable to us.
  • Column budgets are estimates from arXiv HTML, and arXiv versions are routinely longer than camera-ready. Use them as proportions, not targets.
  • The fact-check layer inside the sweep was itself fallible — it “refuted” a correct claim about the ICRA 2024 winners by consulting a page that listed only category awards. Two papers analysed as exemplars (Diffusion Policy, UMI) are influential but not verified award winners. Do not quote award names from this page without checking them.
  • Our riskiest move has thin support. Leading with an honest loss (0.36 vs 0.29 m; 0.80× clean) is backed by two papers, both from strong labs whose authorship may carry the risk in a way ours would not.
