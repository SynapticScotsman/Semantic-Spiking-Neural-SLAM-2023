The frame — what this project actually claims

Every observation is bound to a grid-cell-style position code and added into one 4096-dimensional phasor vector. The map never grows; updating is one addition; merging robots is one addition; unbinding a concept renders a belief field over the floor. We do not claim better accuracy than the state of the art — we claim a measured trade: ~1000× less memory at the accuracy ceiling of our own perception, plus capabilities (exact merge, one algebra for what/where/when) no established map has.

"VSAs are the algebraic composition layer for robotic memory: constant size, one-add updates, exact merging — and we measured that it costs essentially nothing against the perception we feed it."

06 ·

Working: merge — four robots, one addition

The setup: four robots each explore a different quarter of the same room (the four coloured paths below), and each builds its own 32 KB memory, alone. Now their maps must become one map.

How everyone else joins maps: robot 1's map says "couch at (2.0, 1.1)"; robot 2's says "couch at (2.3, 1.3)". Same couch seen from two sides, or two couches? A scene-graph system must decide, for every pair of candidate objects — that decision is called data association. Across our 8-scene test the list-based merge made 622 such decisions, and its wrong ones (merging two real chairs into one, or splitting one couch into two) cost up to 16 points of accuracy on a scene.

How ours joins maps: the four vectors are literally added, number by number. No questions are asked, so none can be answered wrongly — if two robots saw the same couch, their evidence lands on the same spot in the belief field and simply adds up (the field gets brighter there); if they saw different couches, two separate peaks appear. The "10⁻¹²" below means the added-together map is identical, to floating-point dust, to the map one robot would have built by doing all four quarters itself — that is what "exact merge" means. 07 ·

The law underneath — real sweep data, three regimes

Crosstalk χ(N) on controlled synthetic geometry to a million stored items. The mean term never bends — kill it (centring) or scatter it (key diversity) or it kills you. Clean content rides the √N floor; persistent correlation bumps then √-reverts. The quadrature prefactor predicted a held-out cell to 1.4% — and it called the class-imbalance failure in §04 and its fix in advance.

How we got here — eight arcs, in order

  • arc 1
    Centre, don't whiten — scoped.

    Mean-removal carries 40–83% of whitening's benefit by encoder; whitening catastrophic on the classroom (−47.6%, 5 seeds), neutral on chess. Claims scoped, never universal.

  • arc 2
    The capacity law.

    χ(N) = aμN + √N·√(α/PR + γ/hd)·h(N,τ); held-out prediction 1.4%; explains the encoder-independent whitened floor; persistence √-reverts to √(1+τ/τ₀).

  • arc 3
    Key diversity ↔ conditioning are substitutes.

    Yaw-binding rescued bad content on TUM, hurt good content on the classroom (0.35→1.00 m), mildly helped chess's orbit tail — trajectory-dependent, as predicted.

  • arc 4
    The frontend verdict.

    EigenPlaces lifted the classroom ceiling 0.78→0.43 m; the VSA followed to 0.35 m — the memory tracks its ceiling.

  • arc 5
    Dual granularity, measured both ways.

    Objects: crops 0.769 vs place-vectors 0.429. Places: crops 0.96 m vs EigenPlaces 0.35 m. Each wins its own task; both coexist in one trace.

  • arc 6
    Public benchmarks.

    Chess 0.36 m at 32 KB; Replica grounding at the perception ceiling on all 8 scenes; bounded insertion fixed the imbalance failure the law predicted.

  • arc 7
    Merge = addition beats merge = association.

    89%/0-decisions vs 84%/622-decisions; survey: bundling as between-robot map merge appears novel; shared-frame caveat always attached.

  • arc 8
    The metric correction.

    "Don't invent metrics" — bespoke grounding numbers demoted to diagnostics; the student package scores both systems under ConceptGraphs' own eval, honest expectation pre-registered.

12 ·

What we do not claim — say these first

  • Not SOTA accuracy anywhere. DSAC* is 10× better at relocalisation; SAM pipelines label scenes we cannot. The claim is the trade, not the crown.
  • Perception-bound. The failure gallery above is the frontend's, top to bottom.
  • Vocabulary-bound. COCO-80 sees 9–31% of Replica's GT instances.
  • Shared frame assumed. Alignment remains the classical problem; frame-recovery robustness study queued.
  • Bounded capacity, characterised. Beyond the law's crossover, databases win.
  • Seed honesty. 0.35 m (seed-0) and 0.84 m (5-seed) travel together.

A fixed-size vector memory that reaches the accuracy ceiling of its own perception on public benchmarks — ~1000× smaller than the database it replaces, updated in one addition, merged bit-exactly with zero data association, answering where, what and when in a single algebra.
