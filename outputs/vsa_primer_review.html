“Every dimension is a clock” — the original explainer, re-judged against two months of measurement

The Telluride-era demo page taught the whole mechanism with live widgets: position as phase patterns, heading as a circular base, bind/bundle/unbind and their costs, English sentences as keys. That page is preserved unchanged below. This page walks through each of its claims and stamps them with what the benchmarks since did to them: STANDS, SCOPED, or REVISED.

1 ·

Position: every dimension is a clock

the original said

"Every one of the 8192 dimensions is a little clock, each turning at its own fixed rate as you move. No single number holds the position: it is held in the pattern of phases. S(a) ⊗ S(b) = S(a+b) exactly — displacement is native, to floating-point rounding."

stands — and became load-bearing

This homomorphism is now the engine of the two strongest measured results. The VSA Kalman filter (predict = one bind) bounds drift at 0.364 ± 0.051 m over ten seeds because displacement really is one multiply. And the exact-merge property — four robots' maps added, bit-identical to jointly built, 10⁻¹² — is this same exactness at map scale. What the primer showed as a widget curiosity is the paper's sharpest edge.

2 ·

Heading: a circular base, not a ring attractor

the original said

"Heading uses the same fractional power encoding with one change: each dimension gets a random INTEGER frequency k ∈ {−3,−2,−1,1,2,3}. Integer frequencies make the code exactly periodic — turn a full circle and you are back, to floating-point precision, where an ordinary base thinks you are somewhere new. The similarity curve has several lobes (hence the lumpy petals in the replay). No ring attractor network runs anywhere here; the attractor is the natural hardware route for this algebra, not its implementation."

stands — the construction

All still exactly right, including two subtleties worth saying out loud when presenting: (i) the frequency set has no zero — no DC term — which is why marginalising over a full turn of heading annihilates every dimension: "any heading" integrates to nothing rather than to a bias. (ii) The multi-lobe similarity curve is not a bug; it is the price of packing a circle into few integer frequencies, and it is why heading readouts look like petals. The ring-attractor positioning (same family as GC-VSA, Krausse et al. NICE 2025; we compute directly what a CAN would settle into) remains the right careful sentence.

scoped — when to BIND heading at all

What the primer could not know: whether heading should be a factor in the memory turns out to be an empirical, trajectory- and content-dependent decision — measured three ways. On TUM's desk orbit with ill-conditioned crop descriptors, binding heading RESCUED recall (it phase-scatters the descriptor mean — key diversity substituting for conditioning). On the classroom with viewpoint-invariant EigenPlaces content it HURT (0.35 → 1.00 m): the heading factor was pure added crosstalk. On chess's orbit it mildly helped the tail. The rule we now state: content conditioning and key diversity are substitutes — use exactly one rescue. The heading machinery is always available in the algebra; whether you bind it is a measured choice, not a default.

3 ·

Bind, bundle, unbind — and what it costs

the original said

"Three operations are the entire vocabulary. Bundling is a mean: signal falls like 1/k, crosstalk like 1/√(kD), so the margin closes like √(2D/k). That one curve is the capacity of the entire scheme."

scoped — it was the special case

The √(2D/k) law is correct for what the widget simulated: random, clean, uncorrelated atoms. Real encoders broke every one of those assumptions, and the measured capacity law grew three terms the primer's curve cannot see: χ(N) = aμN (a MEAN term — real embeddings share a common direction, and its crosstalk grows linearly and never reverts; kill it by centring or scatter it by key diversity) + √N·√(α/PR + γ/hd) (the primer's floor, now with a prefactor set by the content's participation ratio in quadrature with a projection-noise floor that no treatment can remove) × h(N,τ) (temporally correlated frames add coherently for N≲τ before √-reverting). The old curve is the α/PR-only, τ=0, μ=0 slice. Out-of-sample prediction of the full law: 1.4%. The class-imbalance failure on Replica (1,150 couch observations drowning 5 book observations) is the μ-term at class level — predicted, then fixed by bounded insertion.

4 ·

Whitening: the claim that split in two

the original said

"The whitening is not cosmetic: raw sentence embeddings are strongly anisotropic, and without isotropising them first the projected phasors are too mutually similar to unbind cleanly." (The language pipeline whitened a 241-word vocabulary by PCA before projecting.)

scoped — right for atoms, wrong for streams

Both halves of today's verdict were hiding in this one sentence. For a small, closed ATOM VOCABULARY the primer is still right: atoms must be mutually discriminable, the vocabulary is fixed and tiny, whitening it costs nothing and there is nothing to leak — whiten away. For OBSERVATION STREAMS it is the opposite: whitening the frames going into the memory was catastrophic on the classroom (−47.6% signal across 5 seeds — worse than ignoring the query), roughly neutral on chess, and never better than plain centring/z-scoring anywhere. The law explains why: whitening buys you the removal of the content term α/PR, but the projection floor γ/hd remains (the measured encoder-independent whitened floor), and on streams whitening amplifies noise directions and temporal persistence. The unified rule we now teach: whiten vocabularies, centre streams.

5 ·

One memory, many questions — where / when / what-moved / English

the original said

"One fixed-size memory answering where-given-when, when-given-where and what-changed, all by division and correlation, never by scanning a log. A time range costs about what a time point costs. Abstentions are visible rather than hidden. A phrase the map has never seen lands near the atoms of semantically similar class names and unbinding just works."

stands — now benchmark-tested

The composite query story survived contact with public data: the same algebra ran localisation on 7-Scenes (0.36 m from 32 KB), object grounding on all 8 Replica scenes (at its perception's ceiling, zero memory-attributable misses), and episodic time recall at 11k frames with an exact two-robot time merge. The abstention discipline grew into the z-score/chance-anchor/CDF reporting rules. The English-as-key pipeline is untested at benchmark scale — it remains the honest "demonstrated, not yet scored" item, and its open-vocabulary successor (CLIP relabeling into the Replica vocabulary) is in the student's head-to-head package now.

revised — what the demo could not see

Two things the old page's classroom could never surface. (i) Instance blindness: class ⊗ position gives every chair the same key, so on multi-instance scenes recall is class-correct-but-wrong-instance 72% of the time — the associative fix (each observation's own appearance as the key) nearly doubles instance accuracy, and beyond that, identical twins are a spatial problem no appearance can solve. (ii) The metric lesson: the demo judged itself with its own z-scores; the standing rule now is to adopt the target benchmark's own metric and scorer verbatim — self-defined numbers are diagnostics, never results.

6 ·

Merging two maps is addition

the original said

A one-widget aside: split the walk in half, build two memories, add them, get the joint map back exactly.

stands — and became a first

The throwaway demo grew into the project's most defensible novelty: 4 real sessions and 8 scenes of measured head-to-head where the addition-merge (zero association decisions, 10⁻¹² exact) beats the scene-graph-style associative merge (622 decisions, up to 16 points lost to wrong matches), a literature survey finding no prior use of bundling as a between-robot map merge, and the standing caveat attached: the shared coordinate frame is assumed, not solved.

·

The one-paragraph version for a talk

The 2025 primer taught the mechanism and it all still runs: phases as clocks, heading as an exactly-periodic circular base (no attractor network, no DC term), three operations total. What two months of benchmarks changed is the boundary conditions: the clean-atom capacity curve became a three-term law with a 1.4% out-of-sample prediction; "whitening makes it work" split into whiten-vocabularies / centre-streams; heading-binding went from default to a measured substitute for content conditioning; and the merge aside became the headline. Nothing in the primer had to be retracted — it had to be scoped, which is what measurement is for.
